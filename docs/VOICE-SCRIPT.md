# Clawd's Voice: Recording Script

Clawd talks in **Strinkle**, a made-up gibberish that *sounds* Aussie but isn't English. The
listener should get the feeling from the tune and never catch a real word. That's the Sims trick:
the melody carries the meaning, and the words are just shapes for your mouth.

Every line comes with a rough meaning, so you know what you're *acting*. Don't read the meaning
out. Read the gibberish.

---

## 1. Making it sound Aussie

1. **Rising ending.** Let statements tip up at the end like a question ("Bonzalo?" when you
   mean "Lovely."). This is the single biggest Aussie tell. Use it on about half the lines.
2. **Broad vowels.** Stretch them and open them wide. The `ai` sound slides toward "oi"
   ("mate" → "mite"), `ow` is wide like "cow", and an `-o` on the end becomes "oh-u".
3. **No R at the end.** Endings are "-ah", never "-er". `Rippaloo-ah`, not `Rippaloo-er`.
4. **Lazy consonants.** Soften a T between vowels toward a D: `Roitabo` comes out "roida-bo".
   Don't rush. Clawd is never in a hurry unless something's on fire.
5. **Squash everything into a nickname.** Nearly every word ends in -o, -ie, -za or -roo.
   If you improvise, follow that rule and it'll stay in character.

## 2. Making it sound like Sims chatter

- **Overact the pitch.** Use about twice the up-and-down you'd use in normal speech. The clips
  will be short and the character is small, so subtle won't read.
- **Move while you record.** Shrug, point, slump. It comes through in the voice.
- **If it starts sounding like a real word, mangle it.** Gibberish works because nobody can
  latch on to a word. One accidental "yeah" and the spell breaks.
- **Do three takes of every line:** normal, bigger, smaller. Variety is what stops a 24/7
  room from getting annoying.

## 3. Pronunciation key

Stressed syllables are in CAPS in the word list.

| Spelling | Say it like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `oi` | coin | `roit` = "royt" |
| `ow` | cow | `mowie` = "MOW-ee" |
| `ai` / `air` | air | `yairbo` = "YAIR-boh" |
| `oo` | moon | `strooza` = "STROO-zah" |
| `ee` | see | `greebo` = "GREE-boh" |
| `-o` (end) | oh, drawled | `zappo` = "ZAP-oh-u" |
| `-ah` / `-a` (end) | father | `gidarra` = "gih-DAH-rah" |
| `u` | cup | `blunno` = "BLUN-oh" |

## 11. Recording and export

- **Room:** small and soft. A wardrobe full of clothes or a room with a couch and curtains beats
  a big room with hard walls.
- **Mic:** a hand's width away (15 to 20 cm), a little off to one side so the P's and B's don't
  pop. There are a lot of B's in Strinkle.
- **Format:** record WAV at 48 kHz, 24-bit. Start with a second of silence so you have room tone
  to clean up with.
- **Session flow:** do one mood at a time, all three takes in a row, so you stay in the feeling.
  Drink water. Gibberish dries your mouth out faster than you'd think.
- **Editing:** trim tight to the sound, add a 20 ms fade in and out, and make every clip the
  same loudness (about -16 LUFS).
- **Export:** `.m4a` (AAC) or `.mp3` play reliably in Safari on the iPad.
- **File names:** `<ID>-<take>.m4a`, with takes `a`, `b`, `c`, so `happy-01-a.m4a`,
  `dog-01-c.m4a`, `noise-yawn-b.m4a`. The ID prefixes match the game's mood names and pastime
  ids, so the game can pick a clip without a lookup table.
